# Paper Report 1

This paper is a nothing burger; at least for us. This paper is more oriented towards corporations learning to manage and classify a high volume of autonomous vehicles for customers with varying levels of autonomy. Sure, we could use your scales, but it would be an extreme overkill.

## Relations to OpenUAS

- Table 3 documents pretty much our current avionics set up with the Pixhawk and APM 2.6 (ArduPilot).
  - They say these are airplanes that are less than 20kg.

## Contributions of The Paper

- Introduces the Quantitative Autonomy Metric (UAS AL), a mapping for Human Independence (HI), Mission Complexity (MC), and Environmental Complexity (EC) onto a quantitative 1-10 numerical scale.
- Documents many common configurations of UAS avionics with varying capabilities which debating their effectiveness.
- Analyses the gap between the demand for on-board computational power and what's actually offered by most solutions and concludes that it's a significant concern.
- Recommends a hybrid embedded architecture for mission computers combining multi-core CPUs, embedded GPUs, and tile-based FPGAs with Dynamic Partial Reconfiguration (DPR) interconnected via a Network-on-Chip (NoC).
  - I have no idea what this means and there's no way we get to this level of complexity before the end of the year.

## Methods Worth Using

- Look into OpenPilot CC3D, which they list right beside ArduPilot.
- ROS (Robot Operating System)! We're already working on implementing this!
- OpenCL for getting GPU's working in the air. Would be cool to dedicate one of the LRUs to this as an example in the documentation.
- We should continue upping the compute power of the on-board computers as there's a real-life gap even after the Raspberry Pi.

## Future Works

- Advocates for FPGAs with Dynamic Partial Reconfiguration (DRP) to hot swap functionality.
  - I don't understand what they mean by this since FPGAs are already hot-swappable?
- Points the field towards using power gating FPGAs to achieve lower power consumption by switching off unused cores to reach an oddly specific 10 GFLOPS/W.
- Essentially begs people to implement formal methods to design systems!
- Discrete Controller Synthesis: I don't quite understand this but it analyses bitstreams of FPGAs to check for violations of boundary conditions? I think?
