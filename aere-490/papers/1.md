This paper is a nothing burger; at least for us. This paper is more oriented towards corporations learning to manage and classify a high volume of autonomous vehicles for customers with varying levels of autonomy. Sure, we could use your scales, but it would be an extreme overkill.

## Relations to OpenUAS

## Contributions of The Paper

- Introduces the Quantitative Autonomy Metric (UAS AL), a mapping for Human Independence (HI), Mission Complexity (MC), and Environmental Complexity (EC) onto a quantitative 1-10 numerical scale.
- Documents many common configurations of UAS avionics with varying capabilities which debating their effectiveness.
- Analyses the gap between the demand for on-board computational power and what's actually offered by most solutions and concludes that it's a significant concern.
- Recommends a hybrid embedded architecture for mission computers combining multi-core CPUs, embedded GPUs, and tile-based FPGAs with Dynamic Partial Reconfiguration (DPR) interconnected via a Network-on-Chip (NoC).
  - I have no idea what this means and there's no way we get to this level of complexity before the end of the year.
