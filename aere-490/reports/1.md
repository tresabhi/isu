# AERE 490 Report 1

The first two weeks of the internship were spent primarily fighting with the course registration and banking systems, getting to know the fellow interns, and quizzing the NASA scientists.

There are no public reports for the number of applications this internships gets annually but what I can report is that this year, I was one of the 17 students to be accepted. Most students seem to be in some form of art school.

What surprised me the most is the sheer number of students doing double majors. In fact, I encountered an individual double a double major in Physics and Psychology with minors in some form of clay work and studio art; absolutely fascinating. I am one of the two engineers, the other individual being a Mechanical Engineer in Carolina.

The meeting in week 1 was mostly spent introducing ourselves and going over the specifics and deadlines of the internship. The deadline that I am concerning the most myself is the project proposal due by October 10th.

As for the second meeting, we were introduced to Dr. David Williams, the Psyche-Co Investigator and Deputy Imager Lead. Though it still isn't clear to me what being an Investigator means in the context of a NASA mission but his work on the imaging solutions on board the spacecraft is clear as day.

Dr. Williams gave us a surface level overview of the spectral imager and the expected composition of the surface of the Psyche astroid. Me and the fellow mechanical engineer quizzed Dr. Williams on the specific frequencies of the imager.

Dr. Williams also discussed how the spacecraft starts off with a very high perigee, lowering it over time as it gains more confidence with the gravity of the Psyche astroid. I insisted on more specifics and he mentioned how they use the doppler effect of the satellite's communications with the Earth to detect drifts and create a model of the gravitational field.

The rest of the meeting involved NDA-protected content so I will not be taking a risk sharing information from that. However, the irregular gravity of the astroid got me thinking of the specifics of how this data is collected, stored, and used. I asked Dr. Williams if the density was stored as a big voxel grid or something more simplistic like the center of gravity for which he was unfortunate unable to provide me with the answer.

Since then, I have developed a model in my head. I believe the 3D path of a satellite around an object can be projected to a 2D plane, normal to the satellite and tangential to the center of gravity if the satellite does not orbit the center of mass.
