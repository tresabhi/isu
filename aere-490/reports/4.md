# AERE 490 Report 4

Thanks to the extension that I was kindly provided without a new hard deadline, I was able to focus on getting my schoolwork done before focusing on my painting. With the unprecedented 6 weeks of exams in a row over with, I got around with finalizing the painting.

![](https://i.imgur.com/sajV2as.jpeg)

There we a lot of corners I had to cut to finish this painting in time. Namely, I skipped the atmospherics of Earth like the scattering effect and the haze of the thin atmosphere extending away from the planet into space. The haze effect also exists for the moon which was skipped, as can be seen in this exaggerated example:

![](https://i.imgur.com/Mlp4amXm.png)

Furthermore, a lack of acrylic thinner made the shading on the right surface of the satellite very grainy. I replaced the mount of the radar on the satellite with wrinkled aluminum because the ambient occlusion of the smooth surface of the superstructure was impossible to replicate without the tinner.

The same points can be reiterated for the solar panels. Overall, I, for the first time, did not enjoy painting due to the time constraints. Art is not difficult; it's time consuming. And when you take away the time, you don't get art, you get a disappointment.

Regardless, this is a painting that I have created that I must archive. I will be getting this painting scanned at 16K resolution on Monday much like all other works of mine. Some of my favorite works of art that I have made are at display at the very end of my portfolio: https://abhi-deep.com/.

I don't usually name my paintings but JPL has kindly requested I do. Thus, I have appropriately called it _Farewell_, which both stands for the final goodbye the Psyche satellite gets to send to Earth and my withering hope of ever painting well again.

Changing topics, I have already decided what the next project is going to be. Since I will be traveling for the Thanksgiving and Winter breaks, I have decided to keep things virtual and create a virtual interactive experience that outlines the entire Psyche mission from the launch to the decommissioning with ample opportunities for users to play with the on-board instrumentation and learn things along the way.

The hope is to get this to work as a web-app and then work with the St. Louis Science Museum to get it to work on the big screen for people to use. The source code will be open source and will be available on GitHub at https://github.com/tresabhi/psyche-interactive.
