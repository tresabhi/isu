# AERE 490 Report 6

Hello again! This is week 2 of my second semester taking AERE 4900, but really the reports are cumulative, making this the 6th one. This time, I am working with grazing planetary collisions (relative to the object surface normal), stripping the body of its upper layers. There are many beautiful simulations on this very subject, all rendered onto YouTube.

Here's [NASA's simulation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kRlhlCWplqk) displaying the formation of the moon:

![](https://i.imgur.com/gcCPRLW.png)

[Another video from SciTech Daily](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tb84O-Ik50I) documents a 2D collision of two bodies at a grazing angle closer to what Psyche the asteroid probably encountered.

![](https://i.imgur.com/GbtOm8I.png)

Using Blender shading nodes, I was able to implement accurate black body radiation.

![](https://i.imgur.com/tc75Ttm.png)

This, paired with intentionally broken vertex shaders to pull out parallel vertices around the ring, I was able to attain a strand of ejecting mass form the main mass right after the collision.

![](https://i.imgur.com/7r88O06.png)

With a little bit more work and particle simulation, I should be able to fully replicate the moment Psyche was stripped of its surface.
