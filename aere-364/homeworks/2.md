## 1. (a)

The way I see it is that we're adding three 1-digit integers and outputting them into a 2-digit buffer. And because this is a 3-to-2 transformation, it can be chained into bigger adders, something that I have ironically used heavily in Minecraft.

Another way to look it at is that the actual descriptions of $A$, $B$, or $C$ don't matter as they're being summed. Regardless of where the 1 came from, if the other bits are 0, $O$ will be 1. If there's two 1's, it'll be an overflow with no output. Three 1's lead to both an overflow and output 1.

For $A = 0, 1, 0, 1$ and $B = 1, 0, 0, 1$, the $C$ would be $0, 0, 0, 1$ as the first digit is the only one to have two 1's which gets overflown to the 2nd digit, which, thanks to both 0's from $A$ and $B$ absorbs the overflow, stopping propagation to further digits. Thus, $O$ is $1, 1, 1, 0$ which maps exactly to the table.
