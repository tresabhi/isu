## 1.

Target:

$$
v = \frac{\mu}{h} \sqrt{1 + 2 e \cos \theta + e^2}
$$

The velocity has two components, the transversal and radial velocities. Adding them up should result in the total velocity in the vector format. As for the magnitude, since the equations from Lecture 5 are scalars, the Pythagorean theorem must be used. The velocities respectively are:

$$
v_\theta = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{p}} (1 + e \cos \theta)
$$

$$
v_r = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{p}} e \sin \theta
$$

The magnitude:

$$
v = \sqrt{v_\theta^2 + v_r^2}
$$

$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{p}} \sqrt{(1 + e \cos \theta)^2 + (e \sin \theta)^2}
$$

$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{p}} \sqrt{1 + 2 e \cos \theta + e^2 \cos^2 \theta + e^2 \sin^2 \theta}
$$

$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{p}} \sqrt{1 + 2 e \cos \theta + e^2 \cancel{(\cos^2 \theta + \sin^2 \theta)}}
$$

$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{p}} \sqrt{1 + 2 e \cos \theta + e^2}
$$

This is very close the target but the coefficient needs changing. Fortunately:

$$
p = \frac{h^2}{\mu}
$$

Hence:

$$
\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{p}} = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{\frac{h^2}{\mu}}} = \sqrt{\frac{\mu^2}{h^2}} = \frac{\mu}{h}
$$

Thus:

$$
\boxed{v = \frac{\mu}{h} \sqrt{1 + 2 e \cos \theta + e^2}}
$$

## 2.

Given:

$$
r = [7000, -2000, -4000]^T km
$$

$$
v = [3, -6, 5]^T km/s
$$

$h$ is the specific angular momentum and I am going to need it:

$$
h = r \times v
$$

$$
h = [7000, -2000, -4000]^T \times [3, -6, 5]^T
$$

$$
h = [-34000, -47000, -36000]^T km^2/s
$$

I am also going to need $r$ but normalized:

$$
|r| = \sqrt{7000^2 + 2000^2 + 4000^2} = 8306.62 km
$$

$$
\hat{r} = \frac{r}{|r|} = \frac{1}{8306.62} [7000, -2000, -4000]^T
$$

$$
\hat{r} = [0.8427, -0.2408, -0.4815]^T
$$

Solving for $e$:

$$
e = \frac{v \times h}{\mu} - \hat{r}
$$

Evaluating $v \times h$:

$$
v \times h = [3, -6, 5]^T \times [-34000, -47000, -36000]^T
$$

$$
v \times h = [451000, -62000, -345000]^T km^3/s^2
$$

From Wikipedia for Earth:

$$
\mu = 3.986004418 * 10^{14} m^3/s^2 = 398600.4418 km^3/s^2
$$

$$
\frac{v \times h}{\mu} = \frac{[451000, -62000, -345000]^T}{398600.4418}
$$

$$
\frac{v \times h}{\mu} = [1.13146, -0.155544, -0.865528]^T
$$

Finally:

$$
e = \frac{v \times h}{\mu} - \hat{r}
$$

$$
e = [1.13146, -0.155544, -0.865528]^T - [0.8427, -0.2408, -0.4815]^T
$$

$$
\boxed{e = [0.28876, 0.085256, -0.384028]^T}
$$

It wasn't clear to me if the question wanted the value for $e$ as a scalar or vector so here's the scalar too:

$$
|e| = \sqrt{0.28876^2 + 0.085256^2 + 0.384028^2}
$$

$$
\boxed{|e| = 0.4880}
$$

As for the true anomaly, that's the angle between $e$ and $r$:

$$
\cos \theta = \hat{r} \cdot \hat{e}
$$

$$
\theta = \arccos \hat{r} \cdot \hat{e}
$$

I have $\hat{r}$ already but not $\hat{e}$:

$$
\hat{e} = \frac{e}{|e|} = \frac{[0.28876, 0.085256, -0.384028]^T}{0.4880}
$$

$$
\hat{e} = [0.5917, 0.1747, -0.7869]^T
$$

The product:

$$
\hat{r} \cdot \hat{e} = [0.8427, -0.2408, -0.4815]^T \cdot [0.5917, 0.1747, -0.7869]^T
$$

$$
\hat{r} \cdot \hat{e} = 0.8355
$$

Finally:

$$
\theta = \arccos \hat{r} \cdot \hat{e} = \arccos 0.8355
$$

$$
\boxed{\theta = 0.5818 = 33.33\degree}
$$
