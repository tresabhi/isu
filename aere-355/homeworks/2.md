## 2.

Given:

$$
x_{cg} / \bar{c} = h = 0.3
$$

$$
C_{m_{cg}} = C_{m_0} + \frac{d C_{m_{cg}}}{d C_L} C_L + C_{m_{\delta_e}} \delta_e
$$

$$
C_{m_0} = 0.05
$$

$$
\frac{d C_{m_{cg}}}{d C_L} = -0.1
$$

$$
C_{m_{\delta_e}} = -0.01/\deg
$$

$$
\frac{d C_{m_{cg}}}{d C_L} = \frac{x_{cg}}{\bar{c}} - \frac{x_{np}}{\bar{c}} = h - h_n
$$

$$
\delta_{e_{max}} = \pm 20 \degree
$$

Landing condition:

$$
x_{cg}^{new} / \bar{c} = h^{new} = 0.10
$$

$$
C_L = 1.0
$$

The questions is regarding wether the airplane can be trimmed or not and I have a feeling it can. I will be using the following equation which of course will be $0$:

$$
C_{m_{cg}} = C_{m_0} + \frac{d C_{m_{cg}}}{d C_L} C_L + C_{m_{\delta_e}} \delta_e
$$

$$
0 = C_{m_0} + \frac{d C_{m_{cg}}}{d C_L} C_L + C_{m_{\delta_e}} \delta_e
$$

$$
-C_{m_{\delta_e}} \delta_e = C_{m_0} + \frac{d C_{m_{cg}}}{d C_L} C_L
$$

$$
\delta_e = \frac{1}{C_{m_{\delta_e}}} \left[ -C_{m_0} - \frac{d C_{m_{cg}}}{d C_L} C_L \right]
$$

Everything is known here or given, except the derivative fraction, which is:

$$
\frac{d C_{m_{cg}}}{d C_L} = h - h_n
$$

$$
\delta_e = \frac{1}{C_{m_{\delta_e}}} \left[ -C_{m_0} - (h - h_n) C_L \right]
$$

$$
\delta_e = \frac{(h_n - h) C_L - C_{m_0}}{C_{m_{\delta_e}}}
$$

All I must find is $h_n$ here. This can be found from the initial condition:

$$
\frac{d C_{m_{cg}}}{d C_L} = h - h_n
$$

$$
h_n = h - \frac{d C_{m_{cg}}}{d C_L}
$$

$$
h_n = 0.3 - (-0.1) = 0.4
$$

Back to the original equation:

$$
\delta_e = \frac{(h_n - h^{new}) C_L - C_{m_0}}{C_{m_{\delta_e}}}
$$

$$
\delta_e = \frac{(0.4 - 0.10) * 1.0 - 0.05}{-0.01/\deg} = \boxed{-25\degree}
$$

Well, my instincts were wrong. The trim of $\delta_e = -25\degree < 20\degree$ exceeds the airplane capabilities. I advice the pilot to find the cobra button.

## 3.

Wing:

$$
S = 980ft^2
$$

$$
b = 93ft
$$

Vertical tail:

$$
S_v = 330ft^2
$$

$$
AR_v = 4.3
$$

$$
l_v = 37ft
$$

$$
\eta_v = 1.0
$$

Rudder:

$$
\delta_r = \pm 15 \degree
$$

Propulsion:

$$
T = 14000lbf
$$

$$
y_T = 16ft
$$

Flight condition:

$$
V = 250ft/s
$$

$$
\rho = 0.002378 slug/ft^3
$$

Supplementary information:

$$
\beta = \delta_a = 0
$$

$$
C_{L_{\alpha_v}} = 0.1 \deg^{-1} = 5.73/rad
$$

$$
l_t = y_T = 16ft
$$

The remaining left engine creates a positive moment since $z$ points down:

$$
N_{engine} = T l_t
$$

Sum of moments:

$$
\sum N = 0 = N_{engine} + N = T l_t + N
$$

$$
N = T l_t
$$

Normalizing:

$$
C_n = \frac{T l_t}{q S b}
$$

Definition of $C_n$:

$$
C_n = C_{n_\beta} \beta + C_{n_{\delta_r}} \delta_r + C_{n_{\delta_a}} \delta_a
$$

A few of these angles are $0$:

$$
C_n = \cancel{C_{n_\beta} \beta} + C_{n_{\delta_r}} \delta_r + \cancel{C_{n_{\delta_a}} \delta_a} = C_{n_{\delta_r}} \delta_r
$$

Together:

$$
C_{n_{\delta_r}} \delta_r = \frac{T l_t}{q S b}
$$

Since the goal is to find the minimum size of the rudder, I will be assuming the maximum angle of the rudder is being used. Since the left engine is the only one left, the rudder will assume a positive angle according to the convention:

$$
\delta_r = 15\degree = 0.2618 rad
$$

![](https://i.imgur.com/eq6TIlVm.png)

There exists the rudder control effectiveness:

$$
C_{n_{\delta_r}} = V_v \eta_v C_{L_{\delta_r}}
$$

And since:

$$
\eta_v = 1.0 \implies C_{n_{\delta_r}} = V_v C_{L_{\delta_r}}
$$

Substitution:

$$
V_v C_{L_{\delta_r}} \delta_r = \frac{T l_t}{q S b}
$$

I will take this moment to move some terms around:

$$
q S b V_v C_{L_{\delta_r}} \delta_r = T l_t
$$

$V_v$ is the vertical tail volume coefficient:

$$
V_v = \frac{S_v l_v}{S b}
$$

$$
q \cancel{S b} \frac{S_v l_v}{\cancel{S b}} C_{L_{\delta_r}} \delta_r = T l_t
$$

$$
q S_v l_v C_{L_{\delta_r}} \delta_r = T l_t
$$

And of course $q$ here is the dynamic pressure on the wing:

$$
q = \frac{1}{2} \rho V^2
$$

$$
\frac{1}{2} \rho V^2 S_v l_v C_{L_{\delta_r}} \delta_r = T l_t
$$

$$
\rho V^2 S_v l_v C_{L_{\delta_r}} \delta_r = 2 T l_t
$$

The rudder size lies in the value of $C_{L_{\delta_r}}$ and the equation is getting ugly, so I will solve for $C_{L_{\delta_r}}$ now:

$$
C_{L_{\delta_r}} = \frac{2 T l_t}{\rho V^2 S_v l_v \delta_r}
$$

$$
C_{L_{\delta_r}} = \frac{2 * 14000lbf * 16ft}{0.002378 slug/ft^3 * (250ft/s)^2 * 330ft^2 * 37ft * 0.2618 rad} = 0.943
$$

Now I can use the flap effectiveness parameter:

$$
C_{L_{\delta_r}} = \cancel{\eta_v} \tau C_{L_{\alpha_v}} \frac{S_r}{S_v}
$$

$$
C_{L_{\delta_r}} = \tau C_{L_{\alpha_v}} \frac{S_v}{S}
$$

$$
\tau = \frac{S C_{L_{\delta_r}}}{S_v C_{L_{\alpha_v}}}
$$

$$
\tau = \frac{980ft^2 * 0.943}{330ft^2 * 5.73/rad} = 0.4887
$$

That tau approximately corresponds to about $0.68$.

![](https://i.imgur.com/R1MaGHjm.png)

$$
\frac{S_r}{S_v} \approx 0.68
$$

$$
S_r = 0.68 * S_v
$$

$$
S_r = 0.68 * 330ft^2 = \boxed{224.4ft^2}
$$

$$

Elevator area:

![](https://i.imgur.com/Qjf52JC.png)

The elevator horn is approximately a square:

$$
S_\text{elevator horn} \approx 0.58ft * (1.53ft - 0.75ft) = 0.452ft^2
$$

For the rest of the area, I just drew it in OnShape and measured it:

![](https://i.imgur.com/nv1EER8.png)

$$
S_\text{elevator primary} = 9.004ft^2
$$

Hence:

$$
S_e = S_\text{elevator horn} + S_\text{elevator primary} = 0.452ft^2 + 9.004ft^2 = 9.456ft^2
$$

Getting $\tau$:

$$
\frac{S_e}{S_t} = \frac{9.456ft^2}{54ft^2} = 0.1751
$$

![](https://i.imgur.com/o6kyJPd.png)

$$
\tau \approx 0.38
$$

Formula for $V_H$:

$$
V_H = \frac{l_t S_t}{\bar{c} S}
$$

$$
V_H = \frac{21.6ft * 54ft^2}{7.0ft * 232ft^2} = 0.718
$$

Making $C_{l_{\alpha_t}}$ 3D:

$$
C_{L_{\alpha_t}} = \frac{C_{l_{\alpha_t}}}{1 + \frac{C_{l_{\alpha_t}}}{\pi * AR_t}}
$$

$$
C_{L_{\alpha_t}} = \frac{5.73}{1 + \frac{5.73}{\pi * 4.0}} = 3.936
$$

Formula for $C_{m_{\delta_e}}$:

$$
C_{m_{\delta_e}} = -\eta \tau V_H C_{L_{\alpha_t}}
$$

$\eta$ is assumed to just be a unit value of $1$:

$$
C_{m_{\delta_e}} = -\tau V_H C_{L_{\alpha_t}}
$$

$$
C_{m_{\delta_e}} = -0.38 * 0.718 * 3.936 = \boxed{-1.074}
$$
