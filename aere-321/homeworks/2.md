# AERE 321 Homework 2

## 1.

Given:

$$
\epsilon_z = \gamma_{xz} = \gamma_{yz} = 0
$$

Target:

$$
\sigma_z = \nu (\sigma_x + \sigma_y)
$$

$$
\epsilon_x = \frac{1}{E} [(1 - \nu^2) \sigma_x - \nu (1 + \nu) \sigma_y]
$$

$$
\epsilon_y = \frac{1}{E} [(1 - \nu^2) \sigma_y - \nu (1 + \nu) \sigma_x]
$$

Now for the derivation. For isotropic materials, the following is true:

$$
\epsilon_z = \frac{1}{E} (\sigma_z - \nu (\sigma_x + \sigma_y))
$$

Substitution makes things clearer:

$$
0 = \frac{1}{E} (\sigma_z - \nu (\sigma_x + \sigma_y))
$$

$$
0 = \sigma_z - \nu (\sigma_x + \sigma_y)
$$

$$
\boxed{\sigma_z = \nu (\sigma_x + \sigma_y)}
$$

To reach the second target equation, I will be leveraging the same isotropic equation, just on the $x$ axis:

$$
\epsilon_x = \frac{1}{E} (\sigma_x - \nu (\sigma_y + \sigma_z))
$$

I can now substitute in $\sigma_z$:

$$
\epsilon_x = \frac{1}{E} (\sigma_x - \nu (\sigma_y + \nu (\sigma_x + \sigma_y)))
$$

$$
\epsilon_x = \frac{1}{E} [\sigma_x - \nu \sigma_y - \nu^2 (\sigma_x + \sigma_y)]
$$

$$
\epsilon_x = \frac{1}{E} [\sigma_x - \nu \sigma_y - \nu^2 \sigma_x - \nu^2 \sigma_y]
$$

Factoring out the $\sigma$'s gives us the target equation:

$$
\boxed{\epsilon_x = \frac{1}{E} [(1 - \nu^2) \sigma_x - \nu (1 + \nu) \sigma_y]}
$$

A similar process can be done for the $y$ axis:

$$
\epsilon_y = \frac{1}{E} (\sigma_y - \nu (\sigma_x + \sigma_z))
$$

$$
\epsilon_y = \frac{1}{E} (\sigma_y - \nu (\sigma_x + \nu (\sigma_x + \sigma_y)))
$$

$$
\boxed{\epsilon_y = \frac{1}{E} [(1 - \nu^2) \sigma_y - \nu (1 + \nu) \sigma_x]}
$$
