## 1.

Staring with the inertia:

$$
I = I_x = \int_A y^2 dA = 4 \int_0^a \left[ \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} (a - s) \right]^2 t ~ ds
$$

$$
I = 4 \frac{t}{2} \int_0^a (a^2 - 2as + s^2) ~ ds = 4 \frac{t}{2} \left[ a^2s - as^2 + \frac{1}{3} s^2 \right]_0^a
$$

$$
I = 4 \frac{t}{2} \left[ \frac{1}{3} a^3 \right] = 4 \frac{ta^3}{6} = \frac{2}{3} ta^3
$$

Shear flow of the top right segment:

$$
q_1 = - \frac{V_y}{I_x} \int_0^s t y ~ ds = -t \frac{V_y}{I_x} \int_0^s y ~ ds
$$

$$
q_1 = -t \frac{-V}{\frac{2}{3} t a^3} \int_0^s y ~ ds = \frac{3}{2} \frac{V}{a^3} \int_0^s y ~ ds
$$

Resolving $y$:

$$
y = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} s
$$

$$
q_1 = -t \frac{-V}{\frac{2}{3} t a^3} \int_0^s y ~ ds = \frac{3}{2} \frac{V}{a^3} \int_0^s \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} s ~ ds
$$

$$
q_1 = \frac{3 \sqrt{2}}{4} \frac{V}{a^3} \frac{1}{2} [s^2]_0^2 = \boxed{\frac{3}{4 \sqrt{2}} V \frac{s^2}{a^3}}
$$

The flow at $B$:

$$
q_1(a) = \frac{3}{4 \sqrt{2}} V \frac{a^2}{a^3} = \boxed{\frac{3 V}{4 \sqrt{2} a}}
$$

By inspection, it is easy to see the top left segment is identical to the previous one, but with a shear flow bumped in value by the ending value of the previous segment:

$$
q_2 = q_\text{identical to segment 1} + q_\text{end of segment 1}
$$

$$
\boxed{q_2 = \frac{3}{4 \sqrt{2}} V \frac{s^2}{a^3} + \frac{3 V}{4 \sqrt{2} a}}
$$

Once again, by inspection, it is possible to see the shear flow at $C$ will be double of that at $B$ because we end up recycling the end value of $B$ twice:

$$
q_2(a) = \frac{3}{4 \sqrt{2}} V \frac{a^2}{a^3} + \frac{3 V}{4 \sqrt{2} a} = q_1(a) + q_1(a)
$$

$$
q_1(a) = \boxed{\frac{3 V}{2 \sqrt{2} a}}
$$

And due to symmetry, I can start doodling the shear flow diagram right away:

![](https://i.imgur.com/YQYXJJY.png)

Getting the shear center (sum of moments at the originate):

$$
\sum M = 0 = P e - 2 F
$$

$$
\sum F = 0 = P - V \implies P = V
$$

$$
0 = V e - 2 F
$$

The force:

$$
F = \int_0^a \frac{3}{4 \sqrt{2}} V \frac{s^2}{a^3} ~ ds = \frac{3}{4 \sqrt{2}} V \frac{1}{a^3} \int_0^a s^2 ~ ds = \frac{3}{4 \sqrt{2}} V \frac{\frac{1}{3} a^2}{a^3} = \frac{1}{4 \sqrt{2}} V
$$

$$
0 = V e - 2 \frac{1}{4 \sqrt{2}} V = e - \frac{1}{2 \sqrt{2}}
$$

$$
e = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{4} a
$$

$e$ was defined to the left of the origin, so the shear center is:

$$
\boxed{\left( -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4} a, 0 \right)}
$$

## 2.

Inertia:

$$
I = I_x = \frac{1}{12} t (2a)^3 + \cancel{\frac{1}{12} 2 t^3 a} + t a \left( \frac{a}{2} \right)^2
$$

$$
I = \frac{1}{12} t 8 a^3 + 2 t a \frac{a^2}{4} = \frac{2}{3} t a^3 + \frac{1}{2} t a ^3 = \frac{7}{6} t a^3
$$

The top web comes in from $y=a$ to $y=a/2$, so this is the shear flow:

$$
q_1 = \frac{V}{I} t \int_0^{s_1} y_1 ~ ds
$$

Translating $y_1$ to $s$ (doing top to down):

$$
y_1 = a - s
$$

Thus:

$$
q_1 = \frac{V}{I} t \int_0^{s_1} (a - s) ~ ds
$$

$$
q_1 = \frac{V}{I} t \left( a s_1 - \frac{1}{2} s_1^2 \right)
$$

$$
q_1 = \frac{6}{7} \frac{V}{ta^3} t \left( a s_1 - \frac{1}{2} s_1^2 \right)
$$

$$
\boxed{q_1 = \frac{6}{7} \frac{V}{a^3} \left( a s_1 - \frac{1}{2} s_1^2 \right)}
$$

The shear flow at $A$ due to the top web:

$$
q_{A1} = \frac{6}{7} \frac{V}{ta^3} t \left( a \frac{a}{2} - \frac{1}{2} \frac{a^2}{4} \right)
$$

$$
q_{A1} = \frac{6}{7} \frac{V}{a} \left( \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{2} \frac{1}{4} \right)
$$

$$
q_{A1} = \frac{6}{7} \frac{V}{a} \frac{3}{8}
$$

$$
\boxed{q_{A1} = \frac{9 V}{28 a}}
$$

The top flange comes in from the right, horizontally:

$$
q_2 = \frac{V}{I} t \int_0^{s_2} y_2 ~ ds
$$

$y$ here is constant:

$$
y_2 = \frac{a}{2}
$$

$$
q_2 = \frac{V}{I} t \int_0^{s_2} \frac{a}{2} ~ ds
$$

$$
q_2 = \frac{1}{2} \frac{V}{I} t a s_2
$$

$$
q_2 = \frac{1}{2} \frac{6}{7} \frac{V}{t a^3} t a s_2
$$

$$
\boxed{q_2 = \frac{3}{7} \frac{V}{a^2} s_2}
$$

Shear flow at $A$ due to the top flange:

$$
q_2 = \frac{3}{7} \frac{V}{a^2} a
$$

$$
\boxed{q_{A2} = \frac{3 V}{7 a}}
$$

Using Kirchhoff's junction rule, $q_3$, the shear flow at the top half of the mid web can be shaped:

$$
q_3 = \frac{V}{I} t \int_0^{s_3} y_3 ~ ds + q_{A1} + q_{A2}
$$

$$
y_3 = \frac{a}{2} - s
$$

$$
q_3 = \frac{V}{I} t \int_0^{s_3} \left( \frac{a}{2} - s_3 \right) ~ ds + q_{A1} + q_{A2} = \frac{V}{I} t \left[ \frac{1}{2} as - \frac{1}{2} s^2 \right]_0^{s_3} + q_{A1} + q_{A2}
$$

$$
q_3 = \frac{V}{I} t \left[ \frac{1}{2} as_3 - \frac{1}{2} s_3^2 \right] + q_{A1} + q_{A2} = \frac{1}{2} \frac{V}{I} t s_3 (a - s_3) + q_{A1} + q_{A2}
$$

$$
q_3 = \frac{1}{2} \frac{6}{7} \frac{V}{t a^3} t s_3 (a - s_3) + q_{A1} + q_{A2} = \frac{3}{7} V \frac{s_3}{a^3} (a - s_3) + q_{A1} + q_{A2}
$$

$$
\boxed{q_3 = \frac{3}{7} V \frac{s_3}{a^3} (a - s_3) + \frac{9 V}{28 a} + \frac{3 V}{7 a}}
$$

Flow at $B$:

$$
q_B = q_3(a/2) = \frac{3}{7} V \frac{1}{2} \frac{a}{a^3} \left( a - \frac{1}{2} a \right) + \frac{9 V}{28 a} + \frac{3 V}{7 a}
$$

$$
q_B = \frac{3}{14} V \frac{1}{a^2} \frac{1}{2} a + \frac{9 V}{28 a} + \frac{3 V}{7 a} = \frac{3 V}{28 a} + \frac{9 V}{28 a} + \frac{3 V}{7 a} = \boxed{\frac{6 V}{7 a}}
$$

Taking a moment at the mirror of point $A$ should allow me to exploit the line of action of the flanges and webs:

![](https://i.imgur.com/cUKjY73.png)

Force on the top web:

$$
F_1 = \int_0^{a/2} q_1 ~ ds_1 = \int_0^{a/2} \frac{6}{7} \frac{V}{a^3} \left( a s_1 - \frac{1}{2} s_1^2 \right) ~ ds_1 = \frac{6}{7} \frac{V}{a^3} \int_0^{a/2} \left( a s_1 - \frac{1}{2} s_1^2 \right) ~ ds_1
$$

$$
F_1 = \frac{6}{7} \frac{V}{a^3} \left[ \frac{1}{2} a s_1^2 - \frac{1}{6} s_1^3 \right]_0^{a/2} = \frac{6}{7} \frac{V}{a^3} \left[ \frac{1}{2} a \frac{a^2}{4} - \frac{1}{6} \frac{a^3}{8} \right] = \frac{5}{56} V
$$

The top flange:

$$
F_2 = \int_0^a q_2 ~ ds_2 = \int_0^a \frac{3}{7} \frac{V}{a^2} s_2 ~ ds_2 = \frac{3}{7} \frac{V}{a^2} \int_0^a s_2 ~ ds_2
$$

$$
F_2 = \frac{3}{7} \frac{V}{a^2} \frac{1}{2} a^2 = \frac{3}{14} V
$$

The top half of the mid-web:

$$
F_3 = \int_0^{a/2} q_3 ~ ds_3 = \int_0^{a/2} \left( \frac{3}{7} V \frac{s_3}{a^3} (a - s_3) + \frac{9 V}{28 a} + \frac{3 V}{7 a} \right) ~ ds_3
$$

$$
F_3 = \int_0^{a/2} \left( \frac{3}{7} V \frac{s_3}{a^2} - \frac{3}{7} V \frac{s_3^2}{a^3} + \frac{9 V}{28 a} + \frac{3 V}{7 a} \right) ~ ds_3
$$

$$
F_3 = \left[ \frac{1}{2} \frac{3}{7} V \frac{s_3^2}{a^2} - \frac{1}{3} \frac{3}{7} V \frac{s_3^3}{a^3} + \frac{9 V}{28 a} s_3 + \frac{3 V}{7 a} s_3 \right]_0^{a/2}
$$

$$
F_3 = \frac{1}{4} \frac{1}{2} \frac{3}{7} V \frac{a^2}{a^2} - \frac{1}{3} \frac{3}{7} V
\frac{1}{8} \frac{a^3}{a^3} + \frac{9 V}{28 a} \frac{a}{2} + \frac{3 V}{7 a} \frac{a}{2} = \frac{23}{56} V

Summations:

$$
\sum M = F_1 a - P e = 0
$$

And by symmetry:

$$
\sum F_y = P - 2 (F_2 + F_3) = 0
$$

$$
P = 2 (F_2 + F_3) = 2 \frac{3}{14} V + 2 \frac{23}{56} V = \frac{5}{12} V
$$

Back to the moment:

$$
F_1 a = P e
$$

$$
e = \frac{F_1}{P} a
$$

$$
e = \frac{5}{56} \frac{12}{5} a = \frac{3}{14} a
$$

Thus, the coordinates are:

$$
\left( -e, 0 \right) = \boxed{\left( -\frac{3}{14} a, 0 \right)}
$$

Given:

That diagonal isn't given:

$$
D = \sqrt{R^2 + \left( \frac{H}{2} \right)^2} = \sqrt{(80mm)^2 + \left( \frac{120mm}{2} \right)^2} = 100mm
$$

The left square hole is region $1$ and the remaining triangle is region $2$. Torsion equation:

$$
T = 2 \sum A_i q_i = 2 A_1 q_1 + 2 A_2 q_2
$$

Twist rate:

$$
\frac{d\phi}{dx} = \frac{1}{2 A_i G} \oint \frac{q_i - q_j}{t_i} ds
$$

From region $1$:

$$
\frac{d\phi}{dx} = \frac{1}{2 A_1 G} \left[ \frac{q_1 - q_2}{t_B} H + 2 \frac{q_1}{t_A} L + \frac{q_1}{t_A} H \right]
$$

From region $2$:

$$
\frac{d\phi}{dx} = \frac{1}{2 A_2 G} \left[ 2 \frac{q_2}{t_A} D + \frac{q_2 - q_1}{t_B}H \right]
$$

The areas:

$$
A_1 = LH = 120mm \times 200mm = 0.024 m^2
$$

$$
A_2 = \frac{1}{2} RH = \frac{1}{2} \times 80mm \times 100mm = 0.004 m^2
$$

Plugging this into WolframAlpha would be ideal, but it doesn't support systems of equations with units. Thus, I must make everything into base units and put it into WolframAlpha unitless:

$$
T = 11000 N m
$$

$$
G = 2.71×10^{10} Pa
$$

$$
L = 0.2m
$$

$$
H = 0.12m
$$

$$
D = 0.1m
$$

$$
t_A = 0.005m
$$

$$
t_B = 0.004m
$$

So, the system of equations:

$$
q_1 = x
$$

$$
q_2 = y
$$

$$
d = \frac{d\phi}{dx}
$$

$$
11000 = 2 * 0.024 * x + 2 * 0.004 * y
$$

$$
d = \frac{1}{2 * 0.024*2.71×10^{10}} \left[ \frac{x - y}{0.004} * 0.12 + 2 \frac{x}{0.005} * 0.2 + \frac{x}{0.005} * 0.12 \right] = \frac{1}{2 * 0.004*2.71×10^{10}} \left[ 2 \frac{y}{0.005} * 0.1 + \frac{y - x}{0.004} * 0.12 \right]
$$

This gives:

$$
q_1 = 205292 N/m = 205.3 N/mm
$$

$$
q_2 = 143248 N/m = 143.2 N/mm
$$

$$
\frac{d\phi}{dx} = d = 0.0178442 rad/m = 1.7844×10^{-5} rad/mm
$$

The shear:

$$
q = \tau t
$$

$$
\tau = \frac{q}{t}
$$

From the equation above, it is easy to see that $\tau$ is maximized when $t$ is minimized and $q$ is maximized. Both regions in our assembly share a really thin wall of $t = t_B$. Among the two values for $q$, region $1$ has the higher value:

$$
\tau_{max} = \frac{q_1}{t_B} = \frac{205.3 N/mm}{4mm} = \boxed{51.33 MPa}
$$

And the total twist:

$$
\phi = \frac{d\phi}{dx} * 3m = 1.7844×10^{-5} rad/mm * 3m = \boxed{0.05353rad}
$$

## 2.

The same variables as last time:

$$
T = 11 kN m
$$

$$
G = 27.1 GPa
$$

$$
t_A = 5mm
$$

$$
t_B = 4mm
$$

$$
L = 200mm

The decomposition of the structure will be similar to the worked out example 5 in the week 13 lecture in that there's two identical open parts and just one closed:

![](https://i.imgur.com/tt3JVjn.png)

Summation of torques:

$$
T = T_c + T_1 + T_2
$$

But since:

$$
T_1 = T_2
$$

The previous equation becomes:

$$
T = T_c + 2 T_1
$$

The same goes for the polar moment of inertia:

$$
J = J_c + J_1 + J_2 = J_c + 2 J_1
$$

The polar moment of inertias can be calculated:

$$
A_c = \frac{1}{2} R H = \frac{1}{2} 80mm * 120mm = 48 cm^2
$$

$$
\sum S_i / t_i = 2 \frac{\sqrt{(80mm)^2 + (120mm/2)^2}}{5mm} + \frac{120mm}{4mm} = 70
$$

$$
J_c = \frac{4 A_c^2}{\sum S_i / t_i} = \frac{4 * (48 cm^2)^2}{70} = 132 cm^4
$$

$$
J_1 = J_2 = \frac{1}{3} wh^3 = \frac{1}{3} * 200mm * (5mm)^3 = 0.8333 cm^4
$$

Now, $J_1$ can be safely omitted since $J_1 \ll J_c$, but, I feel bad for the small fella, so it get's in:

$$
J = J_c + 2 J_1 = 132 cm^4 + 2 * 0.8333 cm^4 = 133.7 cm^4
$$

The torques can be resolved now:

$$
T_c = \frac{J_c}{J} T = \frac{132 cm^4}{133.7 cm^4} * 11 kN m = 10.86 kN m
$$

$$
T_1 = T_2 = \frac{J_1}{J} T = \frac{0.8333 cm^4}{133.7 cm^4} * 11 kN m = 0.06856 kN m
$$

The shear flow:

$$
q = \frac{T_c}{2 A_c} = \frac{10.86 kN m}{2 * 48 cm^2} = 1131.3 kN/m
$$

$$
q = \tau t \implies \tau_i = \frac{q}{t_i}
$$

The maximum shear occurs where $t_i$ is minimized which is easy to pick since $q$ is constant:

$$
\tau_\text{max, closed} = \frac{1131.3 kN/m}{4mm} = 282.8 MPa
$$

But for the open parts:

$$
\tau_1 = \tau_2 = \frac{T_1 t_A}{J_1} = \frac{0.06856 kN m * 5mm}{0.8333 cm^4} = 41.138 MPa
$$

Thus, the maximum shear appears on the vertical part of the closed region of the thickness of $4mm$:

$$
\boxed{\tau_{max} = 282.8 MPa}
$$

And since the twist rate is the same for all regions, it's alright to just use:

$$
\phi = \frac{T L}{G J} = \frac{11 kN m * 3m}{27.1 GPa * 133.7 cm^4} = \boxed{0.911rad}
$$
